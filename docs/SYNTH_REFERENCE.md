# Synth Reference

Phase 0.5 research deliverable. Architecture and signal flow of the four
reference instruments, then the DSP foundations the Phase 1 engine is built
on. Sources are cited inline; where sources conflict or a claim is inferred
from behavior rather than documentation, it says so explicitly.

How to use this document: the Sylenth1 section is the DSP blueprint. The
Serum and Vital sections are the blueprint for the wavetable mode and the
modulation UX. The Omnisphere section is the blueprint for the macro layer
only. Nothing here changes params.schema.json directly; recommendations are
collected at the end.

## 1. Sylenth1 (LennarDigital)

The clonable target: every sound is generated by math, no samples.

### Signal flow

Sylenth1 is organized as two Parts, A and B, each containing two oscillators
and one filter, plus a shared master filter stage and a fixed effects chain.
Per the manual and third-party documentation ([manual PDF](https://www.lennardigital.com/files/Sylenth1Manual.pdf),
[Splice overview](https://splice.com/blog/taking-closer-look-lennar-digital-sylenth1-plugin/),
[MusicRadar part 1](https://www.musicradar.com/tuition/tech/how-to-master-sylenth1s-parts-and-oscillators-630710),
[part 2](https://www.musicradar.com/tuition/tech/how-to-master-sylenth1s-filters-and-amplitude-envelopes-630711)):

```
Osc A1 ┐                       ┌ Amp Env A ┐
Osc A2 ┴─> Filter A ──────────>┤           ├─> Mix ─> FX chain ─> out
Osc B1 ┐            (cross-    ┌ Amp Env B ┘
Osc B2 ┴─> Filter B  routable)
```

- Each Part's two oscillator outputs sum into that Part's filter. Filter
  input is cross-routable: oscillators from Part A can feed Filter B and
  vice versa.
- Each filter section is a 4-pole design switchable between 12 and 24
  dB/octave, lowpass / bandpass / highpass, with a drive control that
  saturates inside the filter.
- Two amp envelopes (one per Part), two mod envelopes, two LFOs, and a
  small fixed mod matrix (two user-assignable slots plus hardwired
  destinations for the mod envelopes and LFOs).

### Oscillators and unison

Each of the 4 oscillators produces classic analog waveforms (sine, saw,
triangle, pulse with width, plus noise) with up to 8 unison voices per
oscillator, giving 32 sounding voices per note at full stack. Controls per
oscillator: waveform, volume, pan, octave/note/fine detune, unison voices,
unison detune, stereo spread, phase, invert. Unison detune spreads the
copies symmetrically around the base pitch; combined with stereo spread it
produces the classic wide trance sound. The exact detune distribution
(linear vs weighted toward the edges) is not documented; from listening
comparisons it behaves close to a symmetric spread with center voices
attenuated slightly (inference, not documentation).

### Effects chain

Fixed order, always available, per the manual: arpeggiator, distortion,
phaser, chorus/flanger, equalizer, delay, reverb, compressor. The fixed
order is a feature for us to copy: it removes a whole class of user
confusion and makes golden-render testing tractable.

### Interface

Dense knob panel, one screen, grouped by function (Osc A1/A2, Filter A,
envelopes across the bottom, FX in a tabbed center section). Labels are
short engineering abbreviations (ATK, DEC, SUS, REL, RES). Efficient for
people who already know synthesis, opaque for our target user; we keep the
density and replace the labels (see build plan section 8).

## 2. Serum (Xfer Records)

The wavetable-mode reference. Facts below are from the Serum manual as
shipped with the plugin and Xfer's published materials; version-specific
details refer to Serum 1.x. (Serum 2, released 2025, extends this; not
studied here.)

### Signal flow

Two wavetable oscillators (A, B) plus a dedicated sub oscillator and a noise
oscillator. Osc A/B and noise/sub each have a routing toggle sending them
through the filter or around it. One filter module (many topologies), then a
reorderable effects rack.

### Wavetable design

- A wavetable is a set of up to 256 single-cycle frames; the WT position
  knob scans across frames, and everything can modulate it.
- Warp modes (sync, bend, PWM, FM, AM, ring, formant and others) distort
  the frame being read, which multiplies the timbral range of one table.
- Serum's marquee claim is playback quality: wavetables are resampled with
  very low aliasing even under heavy warp. The manual describes this as
  "ultra clean" oscillators; the implementation is not public. Inference
  from behavior and interviews: heavily oversampled and/or mipmapped
  playback with high-order interpolation.
- Built-in wavetable editor with FFT view: users can draw, import audio,
  and edit harmonics directly.

### Unison, modulation, interface

- Up to 16 unison voices per oscillator with detune, blend (center vs
  edge voice balance), stack modes (octave, fifth) and warp/WT-position
  spread across the stack.
- 3 envelopes and 4 LFOs standard in 1.x (later 1.3x builds allow up to
  8 LFOs; flag: version dependent), all assignable by dragging the
  modulator's icon onto any knob. Drag-to-assign with an on-knob depth
  ring is the modulation UX to copy.
- Effects rack: hyper/dimension, distortion, flanger, phaser, chorus,
  delay, compressor, reverb, EQ, filter; user reorderable, each with a
  mix control, and effects parameters are modulation destinations.

## 3. Vital (Matt Tytel)

The one reference with public source: [github.com/mtytel/vital](https://github.com/mtytel/vital)
(GPLv3), product page [vital.audio](https://vital.audio/). Where a claim
below needs verification during Phase 1, read the source; that is what it
is for.

### Signal flow

Three wavetable oscillators plus a sampler slot, routable individually to
either or both of two filters (parallel or serial), then a fixed-order
effects chain (chorus, compressor, delay, distortion, EQ, filter, flanger,
phaser, reverb; each bypassable, order not user-changeable — inferred from
the UI, verify in source).

### Spectral warping

Vital's oscillator distortions operate in two families: wave warp (time
domain, like bend/sync) and spectral morph (frequency domain: harmonic
stretch, smear, skew, formant scale). Spectral morphing on a wavetable
frame is the "modern" sound Serum cannot fully reach and is worth studying
in source before designing our wavetable mode.

### Filters

Selectable models per filter: analog-style ladder, clean digital SVF
("Clean"), saturated SVF ("Dirty"), diode, formant, comb, and phaser
filters. The SVF implementations are the practical reference for our own
state variable filter; Tytel's code is readable and production-hardened.

### Modulation and interface

- Envelopes, LFOs, and random sources with slots that appear as you use
  them (the UI shows more when needed; secondary sources report counts
  from 4 up to 6 envelopes / 8 LFOs depending on version — flag:
  version dependent, verify in source).
- LFOs are user-drawable curves with grid snapping, rate in Hz or tempo
  sync, and one-shot envelope mode.
- Same drag-to-assign modulation UX as Serum, plus every modulation
  connection has its own bipolar depth and remap curve in a matrix view.
- Real-time visual feedback everywhere: oscilloscopes on oscillators,
  animated filter response, modulation meters on knobs. This is the
  strongest argument that scopes teach beginners; Vital is widely used by
  first-time synth users despite its depth.

## 4. Omnisphere (Spectrasonics)

Studied only for the macro layer and browser. The instrument itself is a
hybrid: a very large sample library ("soundsources") layered with a DSP
oscillator per layer (virtual analog, wavetable, granular), up to four
layers per patch, extensive per-layer filters/envelopes/LFOs, and effects
racks. Source: Spectrasonics' published reference guide and product
documentation. The part users recognize is the library; that is why it is
not a DSP blueprint (build plan section 0).

What we take from it:

- **The macro layer.** The Orb is a single X/Y-ish control that moves many
  underlying parameters at once in a musically-curated way. Most users
  never leave it. Our eight macro knobs are the same idea with legible
  names.
- **Attribute-first browsing.** Patches are browsed by mood, genre, and
  type keywords rather than synthesis taxonomy. Our preset browser should
  be organized by vibe ("Warm pad"), not signal path ("2-osc LP24 pad").

## 5. DSP foundations

References used throughout: Hal Chamberlin, *Musical Applications of
Microprocessors* (SVF); Vadim Zavalishin, *The Art of VA Filter Design*
(TPT/trapezoidal filters); Andrew Simper's Cytomic technical papers
(trapezoidal SVF); Stilson & Smith, *Analyzing the Moog VCF with
Considerations for Digital Implementation* (1996); Huovilainen, *Non-linear
digital implementation of the Moog ladder filter* (DAFx 2004); Välimäki,
Nam, Smith & Abel, *Perceptually informed synthesis of bandlimited
classical waveforms* / PolyBLEP literature (c. 2010); Karplus & Strong
(1983) for comb-based string synthesis.

### 5.1 State variable filter

The classic Chamberlin digital SVF, per sample with input `x`, tuning
`f = 2 sin(pi * fc / fs)` and damping `q = 1/Q`:

```
low  = low + f * band
high = x - low - q * band
band = band + f * high
notch = high + low
```

Outputs lowpass, highpass, bandpass, and notch simultaneously, which is why
an SVF is the right single filter for our schema's `filter_type` switch.
Caveat: the Chamberlin form loses accuracy and stability as `fc` approaches
`fs/6`; the standard fix is oversampling it 2x, or better, using the
trapezoidal (TPT) SVF from Zavalishin/Simper, which is stable and accurate
across the full audio band:

```
g = tan(pi * fc / fs)        k = 1/Q
a1 = 1 / (1 + g * (g + k))
a2 = g * a1
a3 = g * a2
v3 = x - ic2eq
v1 = a1 * ic1eq + a2 * v3
v2 = ic2eq + a2 * ic1eq + a3 * v3
ic1eq = 2 * v1 - ic1eq
ic2eq = 2 * v2 - ic2eq
low = v2   band = v1   high = x - k * v1 - v2
```

Phase 1 should implement the TPT form directly (it is barely more code) and
the filter response test in the harness will verify the knee and resonance
peak land where the schema says.

### 5.2 Ladder filter

The Moog ladder is four one-pole lowpass stages in series with negative
feedback `k` (0 to 4) from the output back to the input; self-oscillation
at k = 4. Digital naive form per Stilson & Smith, with per-stage
`g = 1 - exp(-2 * pi * fc / fs)` (one-pole coefficient):

```
u = x - k * y4
y1 += g * (tanh(u)  - tanh(y1))
y2 += g * (y1 - y2)      # Huovilainen: tanh() on every stage
y3 += g * (y2 - y3)
y4 += g * (y3 - y4)
```

The tanh saturations are what give the ladder its character under drive
(Huovilainen 2004). A delay-free-loop correction or zero-delay feedback
solver is needed for accurate tuning at high cutoff. Not in the Phase 1
schema; listed as a future `filter_type` option in the recommendations.

### 5.3 Comb filter

Feedforward: `y[n] = x[n] + g * x[n - M]`; feedback:
`y[n] = x[n] + g * y[n - M]`. Delay length M sets the tooth spacing
`fs / M`. Feedback combs with interpolated fractional delay are the core of
chorus/flanger (M modulated by an LFO) and Karplus-Strong strings (M from
pitch, with a lowpass in the loop). Relevant to the Phase 3 effects chain,
not the Phase 1 filter.

### 5.4 Anti-aliasing: PolyBLEP and mipmapped wavetables

A trivially-generated saw or square contains harmonics above Nyquist that
fold back as inharmonic tones. Our harness proves this instantly (the
naive-saw negative control fails at -54 dBFS of alias energy).

**PolyBLEP** (polynomial bandlimited step): keep the naive waveform but
replace the neighborhood of each discontinuity with a polynomial correction
spanning the two samples around it. With `t` = phase in [0,1), `dt` =
phase increment per sample, the standard 2-sample correction added to a
step edge at phase 0 is:

```
if t < dt:        # just after the edge
    u = t / dt;   correction = -(u*u)/2 + u - 1/2 ... (sign per edge)
elif t > 1 - dt:  # just before the edge
    u = (t - 1) / dt; correction = (u*u)/2 + u + 1/2
```

Concretely: saw = naive saw minus polyblep(t, dt); square = difference of
two polybleps half a period apart (which also gives variable pulse width
for free). Triangle is generated by integrating the corrected square.
PolyBLEP suppresses aliasing far below the -60 dBFS test line for classic
waveforms at melodic pitches and costs almost nothing per sample. This is
the Phase 1 oscillator algorithm.

**Mipmapped wavetables** are the equivalent for arbitrary tables: store
each wavetable frame at multiple bandlimits (typically one per octave, each
containing only harmonics below Nyquist for its pitch range), pick the
table for the current pitch, and interpolate (linear between mip levels,
cubic within a table, or better). This is the wavetable-mode algorithm and
also how Serum-class quality is reached; Vital's source shows a production
implementation.

### 5.5 ADSR curve shapes

Human loudness perception is roughly logarithmic, and natural decays
(strings, plucks, rooms) are exponential in amplitude. A linear amplitude
decay therefore sounds wrong in a characteristic way: it seems to hold too
loud for too long and then fall off a cliff at the end. Decay and release
must be exponential:

```
level *= exp(-1 / (tau * fs))      # one multiply per sample
```

with `tau` derived from the knob time (a common convention: knob time =
time to fall within ~0.1% of target, i.e. 6.9 tau). Attack is different:
a pure exponential approach never arrives and sounds slow and mushy; the
analog-modeled convention is an exponential charge toward an overshoot
target (e.g. aiming at 1.3 and clamping at 1.0), which reads as a fast,
slightly rounded linear ramp. Envelope segment transitions (note-on during
release, retrigger) must ramp from the current level, never jump; the
harness click test exists precisely for this.

### 5.6 Voice allocation and stealing

For 16-voice polyphony:

- Keep voices in a pool with states: idle, attacking, sustaining,
  releasing.
- Note-on: take an idle voice; if none, steal. Steal priority (standard
  practice across synths, and audible-artifact-minimizing): first a
  releasing voice (quietest first), then the oldest sustaining voice,
  never the most recent note.
- Stealing must not click: either apply a very fast fade (1 to 5 ms) on
  the stolen voice before reuse, or keep a tiny crossfade pool. The
  harness click test at voice-steal boundaries enforces this (M2 "voice
  stealing is inaudible").
- Same-note retrigger policy: mono-per-note (retrigger the existing
  voice's envelopes) sounds tighter for our target user than stacking
  duplicates (inference from common practice; Sylenth1 offers modes).

## 6. Recommendations for params.schema.json

Phase 0.5 does not modify the schema. Listed for the Phase 1/2 owners to
adopt deliberately (each addition automatically flows to UI, LLM schema,
optimizer bounds, and preset format via codegen):

1. `filter_drive` (float, log-ish taper): saturation inside the filter is
   a large part of the Sylenth sound and of "warm"/"gritty" vocabulary.
2. `filter_slope` (enum: 12, 24 dB/oct): cheap in an SVF (cascade two),
   large timbral payoff, expected by users coming from any reference synth.
3. `osc*_phase` + `osc*_phase_random` (or a retrigger enum): needed for
   punchy basses (fixed phase) vs lush pads (random phase); also affects
   golden-render determinism, so decide before M2. Until then the engine
   must default to deterministic phase or golden tests cannot be bit-exact.
4. Noise source: either `osc_noise_level` as a third source or a waveform
   option; needed for "airy", "breathy", percussion transients.
5. `glide_time` (float, log): portamento; essential for leads and 303-style
   basses.
6. LFO additions: `lfo_sync` (enum: hz, tempo divisions), `lfo_retrigger`
   (enum: free, note), `lfo_delay`/`lfo_fade` (float): vibrato that fades
   in is a top-5 request in NL terms ("add some vibrato").
7. A second LFO and 2 to 4 generic mod matrix slots
   (source/destination/amount), once the mod routing design exists.
8. Effects parameters (Phase 3, fixed chain per Sylenth): chorus
   rate/depth/mix, delay time/feedback/mix (with tempo sync), reverb
   size/decay/mix, distortion drive/mix, simple 2-band EQ. The sound
   vocabulary constantly wants "space", "wet", "echo"; without these the
   NL layer cannot satisfy a third of common requests.
9. `master_width` (float 0..2): mid/side scaling; "wider"/"mono" requests
   need a master control in addition to unison detune.
10. `amp_velocity` (float 0..1): velocity-to-level sensitivity; keyboards
    feel dead without it once real synthesis exists.
